# Travel Context — Evergreen Family Trip Preferences

This file holds the standing preferences for planning trips with this family. It does not change trip to trip — trip-specific details (homebase, dates, trip length) are gathered fresh each time by the `/plan-trip` skill and are never written back here.

## Family

- Two adults, two children:
  - Boy, born 2016
  - Girl, born 2019
- Plan activities that work across this age range — nothing requires reading fluency, long lecture-style tours, or adult-length attention spans, but nothing has to be dumbed down either. Ruins, art, and nature sites that reward curiosity and hands-on exploration are ideal.

## Mobility & Activity Limits

- **Hiking cap:** total hiking on any given day must stay under 5 miles.
- **Radius:** the family is mobile with a car — anything within a 90-minute drive of the homebase city is in scope.
- **Lodging:** the family stays in a house (not a hotel). Never suggest or research hotel options — lodging is already handled.

## Trip Pace

Always account for both paces when building itinerary options, and label each option accordingly:
- **Relaxed** — fewer stops, built-in downtime, shorter drives, room to linger.
- **Packed** — maximizes activities per day, tighter scheduling, more ground covered.

## Food Philosophy

- No dietary restrictions.
- **Fish/seafood spots are a standing favorite** — actively surface strong local seafood options when they exist near a site or homebase.
- Bias hard toward **authentic, local food and activities over tourist-trap or marquee attractions**. Given a choice between the obvious headline attraction and a well-regarded local alternative, prefer the local one.
  - Example: in Paris, prefer Palais de Tokyo or a local gallery over suggesting the Louvre.
  - Apply the same logic to restaurants: neighborhood spots and local favorites over attraction-adjacent tourist restaurants.

## Research Priorities

When researching a destination, prioritize findings in these categories:

1. **Land Art & Sites** — large-scale land art, outdoor sculpture parks, or site-specific installations.
2. **Archaeology & History** — ruins, castles, or archaeological sites that are interactive or "ruin-like" enough to capture a child's imagination (climbable, explorable, tactile — not glass-case museum exhibits). Also include gemstone mining experiences (e.g., https://www.bootsandbirdies.com/gemstone-mining) as a strong category fit.
3. **Active Nature** — flat-water kayaking/canoeing spots and easy biking trails, especially ones that connect to historic or art sites from #1 and #2.
4. **Festivals & Culture** — music and cultural events happening during the specific travel window (this requires live/current research each time, not general knowledge).

## What NOT to Suggest

- Hotels or lodging of any kind.
- Headline tourist attractions when a comparable authentic/local alternative exists.
- Hikes or itineraries that push total daily hiking over 5 miles.
- Sites or day plans outside the 90-minute driving radius from the homebase.
